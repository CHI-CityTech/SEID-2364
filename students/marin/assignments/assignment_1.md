### 3. Interpretive Caution

Although the dataset is rich, it has important limitations.

#### What the Dataset Can Support

- It can reveal descriptive patterns: what people tended to choose in specific scenarios.
- It can show cross‑cultural variation in moral intuitions.
- It can highlight common themes such as harm minimization, fairness, or protection of the vulnerable.

These descriptive insights are valuable for understanding how people react to moral dilemmas.

#### What the Dataset Cannot Support

- It cannot determine what is morally right. Descriptive data does not produce normative truth.
- It cannot claim to represent global moral consensus, because participation was self‑selected.
- It cannot reliably predict how people would behave in real emergencies, where emotions, context, and stakes differ dramatically.
- It cannot justify policy decisions simply because "most people chose X."

#### Where Aggregation Hides Conflict

Aggregating millions of responses smooths out disagreement. For example:

- Some cultures strongly prioritize the young; others emphasize equality across ages.
- Some groups reject the idea of valuing law‑abiding individuals more than law‑breakers.
- Some participants may have clicked randomly or treated the task as a game.

A single global average hides these tensions and may create the illusion of consensus where none exists.

### 4. Ethics of Using the Dataset

Should crowd‑sourced moral data influence real‑world systems like autonomous vehicles?

A cautious answer is: **only in limited ways and under strict safeguards.**

#### Arguments for Limited Use

- Public input can help identify which moral questions matter to people.
- Patterns can reveal which dilemmas require deeper ethical analysis.
- The dataset can highlight areas where public expectations conflict with expert norms.

#### Arguments Against Direct Use

- Encoding majority preferences into machines risks reinforcing bias.
- Minority groups could be systematically disadvantaged if their moral intuitions differ from the majority.
- Ethical decisions in life‑and‑death contexts should not be determined by popularity.

#### Reasonable Safeguards

If the dataset is used at all, it should be:

- **Advisory, not prescriptive.**
- **Combined with ethical theory, legal standards, and human rights principles.**
- **Interpreted with transparency,** acknowledging uncertainty and bias.
- **Reviewed by diverse stakeholders,** not just engineers.

In short, the dataset is a useful tool for ethical reflection, but it should not dictate machine behavior.
